Corrected September 2026 — please read before the results

A re-analysis in September 2026 withdrew several headline claims made on this page. The hardware data is unchanged and was not the problem; the statistics applied to it were.

  • The “coupling ratio” divided by a baseline that is exactly zero in theory, so it measured one over the noise floor. It scaled with the square root of the shot count and differed between backends for that reason alone.
  • The universality result (r = 0.89) was a correlation that unrelated curves of the same rise-and-fall shape reach about one time in six. The two curves peak at different couplings and fail a scaling-collapse test.
  • The metric claim rested on two tests that could not fail on any input. On a real test the triangle inequality is violated for roughly one triple in six, and the construction is a semi-metric, not a metric.
  • The universal gravitational coupling (r = 0.9987) was definitional. Both series carry the same imposed 1/λ factor, and measuring nothing at all scores a perfect 1.0000 on the same test.
  • “Spacetime tearing” does not remove entanglement. After the coupling stops the circuit applies only local operations, which cannot change entanglement across the chain cut. The correlator collapses while the entanglement is mathematically frozen.

What survives is one reproducible result: coupling the chains produces a cross-field correlation well clear of the noise, at 8, 16 and 128 qubits on two processors. The geometric interpretation built on top of it is not supported by this data. Sections below are annotated individually.

01 — The Question

Is spacetime made of entanglement?

In 2010, physicist Mark Van Raamsdonk proposed a radical idea: spacetime isn't fundamental. It emerges from quantum entanglement between fields. Remove the entanglement, and spacetime tears apart.

This idea remained largely theoretical. We built a framework to probe it on real quantum hardware.

Note on what follows: testing Van Raamsdonk's proposal requires measuring entanglement. Experiments 1 to 8 measured a single-basis correlation function, which cannot tell entanglement from ordinary classical correlation. A measurement that can was added in September 2026 and has not yet been run on hardware.

🔗

Entanglement

Quantum correlations between particles that persist regardless of distance. Einstein called it "spooky action at a distance."

🌌

Spacetime

The fabric of the universe — the stage on which all physics plays out. General relativity describes its curvature.

❓

The Connection

If entanglement creates spacetime, then quantum computers — which create entanglement on demand — offer a place to look for the signature in miniature.

03 — The Experiments

Eight experiments. One framework.

Over five days, we ran eight hardware experiments scaling from 8 to 128 qubits, testing different Hamiltonians, measurement bases, and architectures. A ninth, measuring entanglement directly, was added in September 2026 and has not yet been run.

Experiment 1

The Lambda Sweep

Vary coupling strength from 0 to 2.0. Cross-chain correlations grow with it, reaching |C| ≈ 0.17 against a decoupled baseline consistent with zero.

Corrected: the “27.9× above baseline” figure is withdrawn. The baseline is a noise floor, so the ratio scaled with shot count rather than with physics.

Experiment 2

The Null Hypothesis

Run a single chain without a partner. The cross-chain structure is much weaker, as expected if the signal requires two coupled fields.

Not re-examined. The quoted 10-20× is a ratio of the same kind withdrawn elsewhere and should be treated as provisional.

Experiment 3

Universality Test

Ising (ZZ) vs Heisenberg (ZZ+XX) coupling. Both produce a rise-then-fall correlation curve, but not the same curve: they peak at λ = 0.75 and λ = 1.5.

Corrected: r = 0.89 is withdrawn as evidence. Unrelated curves of that shape reach it about one time in six, and a scaling-collapse test rejects universality at χ²/dof = 9.0.

Experiment 4

16-Qubit Scale

Double the system size to 8+8. Geometry persists and strengthens. The framework scales.

Experiment 5

128-Qubit Full Chip

Use 128 of 133 qubits on IBM Torino. 16 independent experiments running simultaneously. Geometry emerges across the entire processor.

Experiment 6

Multi-Basis Discovery

Measure in Z, X, and Y bases. Each basis reveals a different geometric component. The Hamiltonian symmetry determines which direction is strongest.

Experiment 7

Metric Tensor Extraction

Combine three bases into a diagonal correlation tensor and test it against the metric axioms.

Corrected: this is not a metric. Positive definiteness holds by construction and cannot fail. The 100% triangle result came from a test that returns 100% for any input, including random numbers. On a real site-to-site test, 83.9% of 168 triples are satisfied and the identity of indiscernibles fails.

Experiment 8

Gravitational Analysis

Apply Jacobson's derivation, G_eff = 1/(4λC), to each tensor component.

Corrected: the universal coupling (r = 0.9987) is withdrawn. Both series carry the same imposed 1/λ factor, so replacing both measurements with constants still scores 1.0000. Correlating only the measured part gives r = 0.63, which unrelated curves match about half the time.

05 — The Metric Tensor

The correlations are anisotropic.

Measuring in three bases (Z, X, Y) gives a diagonal correlation tensor whose components differ by direction, and the anisotropy follows the coupling term.

Corrected: this tensor is not a metric. The identity of indiscernibles fails, and the triangle inequality is violated for roughly one triple in six. A ZZ coupling producing ZZ correlations is also what the Hamiltonian is built to do, so read this as confirmation that the circuit behaves as designed rather than as a discovery about geometry. All six coupling-and-basis combinations are statistically non-zero; the weaker ones are suppressed, not absent.

Both series are one over λ times a measured quantity, so they share an imposed 1/λ factor. Replacing both measurements with constants, which measures nothing at all, scores a perfect r = 1.0000 on the same test, so the published figure sits below the no-information ceiling. Correlating only the measured input gives r = 0.63 with a 95% interval of 0.43 to 0.77, which unrelated curves match about half the time. There is no evidence here for a universal coupling.
07 — Key Findings

What survives, and what did not.

Each finding below carries its status after the September 2026 re-analysis: two stand, two are restated, four were never re-examined and are provisional, four are withdrawn, and one is new and awaiting hardware.

Finding 01 — stands

Coupling produces a cross-field correlation

|C| reaches 0.07 to 0.17 against a decoupled baseline that is exactly zero in theory. The signal is roughly twelve times the shot-noise scale and reproduces at 8, 16 and 128 qubits on two processors. This is the one result the project rests on.

Finding 02 — restated

Decoupling suppresses the correlator without removing entanglement

The reduction is 84.3% (95% CI 75.7 to 90.9) on the 8-qubit run, leaving a small residual that simulation predicts. It is not evidence for Van Raamsdonk's disconnection: after the coupling stops the circuit applies only local operations, which cannot change entanglement across the cut. Simulation confirms the entanglement is unchanged to 1e-14 bits while the correlator falls 88%.

Finding 03 — provisional

Two fields appear to be required

A single chain shows much weaker cross-chain structure. The quoted 10-20× is a ratio of the kind withdrawn elsewhere and has not been re-computed.

Finding 04 — withdrawn

Universality across Hamiltonians

Ising and Heisenberg do not produce the same curve. They peak at λ = 0.75 and λ = 1.5 and fail a scaling-collapse test at χ²/dof = 9.0, with the worst point 4.1σ off. The r = 0.89 reflects a shared rise-and-fall shape that unrelated curves reproduce about one time in six.

Finding 05 — stands

The signal reproduces from 8 to 128 qubits

Measured on IBM Torino and IBM Fez at three system sizes. The absolute correlation is consistent across them; only the withdrawn ratio varied.

Finding 06 — restated

The correlations are anisotropic

Z-coupling gives Z-dominant correlations and XY-coupling gives X-dominant ones. All six coupling-and-basis combinations are statistically non-zero, so the signal is suppressed off-axis rather than absent, and the earlier “visible / invisible” framing was wrong. A ZZ term producing ZZ correlations is what the Hamiltonian is built to do.

Finding 07 — withdrawn

Positive definite metric

The tensor is a diagonal of absolute correlation values, so every eigenvalue is non-negative by construction. The test cannot fail on any input and is not evidence.

Finding 08 — withdrawn

100% triangle inequality satisfaction

That test compared scalars on a line using |x₁ − x₂|, which satisfies the triangle inequality as an algebraic identity. It returns 100% for random numbers. On a real 8×8 site-to-site distance matrix, 83.9% of 168 triples are satisfied, and the identity of indiscernibles fails, so the construction is a semi-metric.

Finding 09 — provisional

Geometric phase transition

The λ ≈ 0.31 inflection is a second derivative of an 8-point sweep. Re-sampling with shot noise alone puts its 95% interval at 0.04 to 1.58, which spans the whole measured range, and 5% of resamples show no crossing at all.

Finding 10 — provisional

Eigenvalue evolution

Flatness drops from 0.84 to 0.07 across the sweep. Not re-examined, and it inherits the uncertainty of the underlying per-basis values.

Finding 11 — provisional

Direction-dependent effective constant

G_eff varies across directions because the measured correlations do. The variation is inherited from Finding 06 rather than being an independent result.

Finding 12 — withdrawn

Universal gravitational coupling

The r = 0.9987 is definitional. Measuring nothing at all scores 1.0000 on the same test. The measured part alone gives r = 0.63 and fails a scaling collapse at χ²/dof = 16.5.

Finding 13 — added, not yet run on hardware

An actual entanglement measurement

Experiment 9 adds local randomized measurements and an entanglement witness, which certifies entanglement even when the device state is mixed. In simulation of the same circuits the coupled and torn states are both certified entangled while the decoupled state is correctly not. The torn state is the point: its correlator has collapsed to about a ninth of the coupled value and its entanglement is intact. This has not been run on a device.

08 — What's Next

One measurement comes first.

The re-analysis leaves a clear ordering. Everything below is speculative until the entanglement measurement is actually run, because without it the project has no observable that can distinguish entanglement from ordinary correlation.

🎯

Run the entanglement experiment

Experiment 9 is written, validated against exactly known values, and checked against a separable control that it correctly declines to certify. It needs 2000 random measurement bases per state on a device. Until it runs, every entanglement number in this project is simulation. This is the only step that can move the results, and the only one that should be taken next.

💾

Archive the raw counts

The stored results hold derived summaries only, so none of the hardware analysis can be independently re-run or checked. Re-archiving the bitstring counts is the cheapest fix and the largest single gain in credibility available.

⚛️

Cross-Platform Validation

Run the same experiments on trapped-ion hardware. If the correlation reproduces on atoms instead of wires, it is a property of the physics rather than of one device.

💥

Big Bang Experiment

Ramp coupling from zero across Trotter steps. Watch spacetime emerge from nothing. Measure the curvature history of a toy universe from birth to maturity.

🕳️

Black Hole Analog

Create a spatial gradient in coupling — strong center, weak edges. Look for a horizon where geometry changes character.

🌊

Gravitational Waves

Oscillate coupling sinusoidally. If the oscillation propagates along the chain with delay, that's a propagating disturbance in emergent geometry.
